Lesson 6

Create with Integrity — Ethics, Copyright, and Original Work

Step 6.11 of 6

Step 6.1 · Start here

AI can help people draft text, write code, make images, and summarize information. But people must still make important legal, workplace, and ethical choices. AI-made content is not always free to use or fully original. It may also break copyright, trademark, or workplace rules. You must check the source, permission, and rules before using or sharing it.

In this lesson, you will learn about copyright, fair use, public domain work, licenses, ownership, credit, and honesty about AI use. You will review school and workplace examples. You will decide whether someone has permission, how to give credit, when to explain AI use, and what action is fair.

💡 Big Question and Core Rule

Big Question: How can people use AI to create and share work in a fair and legal way? How can they respect copyright, follow license rules, and show their own original work?

Main Rule: Respect the rights of creators and be honest. Check permission, give correct credit, and explain AI use when required. Make sure a person's own work guides the final result.

🧠 Memory Phrase: Verify rights. Give credit. Disclose AI. Own the outcome.

🛡️ Use the Responsible AI Routine

Use this pathway whenever AI may support a task.

👀 Your next move: Study the Responsible AI Routine below. Before sharing AI-assisted work, check copyright, give credit, and explain how AI helped.

Goal → Protect → Use → Check → Own

  • Goal: What creative, academic, or workplace product is being developed?
  • Protect: What copyrighted material, trademarks, or proprietary content must be protected from improper use?
  • Use: Is the input content licensed, public domain, or approved for use within AI tools?
  • Check: Does the AI-assisted work require proper attribution, license compliance, or trademark safeguards?
  • Own: Has the AI usage been clearly disclosed, and is a human creator taking full ownership of the final work?

🎯 Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, students can:

  • Explain copyright, permission, original work, credit, trademarks, and honesty about AI use.
  • Distinguish fair use, permissive licensing, public domain, and restricted commercial use.
  • Identify potential intellectual property, copyright, and trademark concerns in AI outputs.
  • Write clear source credits and AI-use statements that follow school or workplace rules.
  • Evaluate a scenario to choose an ethical and legal path for using AI-supported work.
✅ You will show success by: identifying legal/ethical limits of AI outputs, writing accurate source attributions, disclosing AI assistance properly, and justifying an ethical workflow for content creation.

📚 Key Words to Know

Legal Protections & Rights
Copyright: Legal protection for original work created by people and saved in a tangible form. It gives owners specific rights over how work is used.
Permission: Approval to use, copy, change, upload, or share someone else's work or information.
Trademark: A word, name, symbol, or design that identifies where goods or services come from. Logos or branding similar to existing trademarks create legal concerns.
Ethics & Transparency
Originality: The use of your own ideas, choices, and creative work instead of copying someone else.
Attribution: Naming the creator and source of work you used and following credit rules in its license, terms, or professional requirements.
Transparency: Clearly explaining when, why, and how AI helped create the final work.

Connect & Learn: Ethics and Human Responsibility

Estimated time: 8 minutes

Scenario

A marketing student asks AI to make a logo that looks almost exactly like a famous brand. The student sends the first result to a real client without checking it or saying that AI was used. When questioned, the student says, “AI made it, so I am not responsible.”

Respond to yourself:

• Who could be affected by this decision?

• Whose work, identity, or reputation may be involved?

• Who must take responsibility for checking and approving the final logo?

📖 Your next move: Continue with the short reading and examples below.

Ethics are principles people use to decide what is right, fair, and responsible. AI can help create useful work, but it cannot accept blame or make moral choices. People choose the goal, data, prompt, and final use.

An ethical review starts with the people who may be affected. Ask who may benefit, who could be harmed, and whose point of view may be missing. Then check permission. Did the creator allow the work, image, or idea to be used? Did the person allow their data to be used? Finally, identify who must check the result and make the final decision.

A fast or polished AI response does not remove human responsibility. Students, workers, schools, and employers must follow rules, fix problems, and stop harmful uses. In this lesson, focus on the creator’s purpose, permission, review, and final decision. Do not ask only, “Can AI do this?” Also ask, “Should we use it this way?”

Learn: Copyright and Permission in AI-Created Work

📖 Your next move: Continue with the short reading and examples below.

Copyright protects original work created by people and recorded in a form that can be seen, heard, or saved. This includes writing, art, music, photos, video, and code. Before using material, identify its source and check the permission, license, contract, or terms. Make sure you are allowed to upload, copy, change, or share it. Copyright rules can be complex, so ask a qualified adult when the answer is unclear.

AI can make source and rights questions harder. Training data may include work created by people. Users may upload material they do not have permission to share. An AI result may also look similar to existing work. Similarity alone does not answer every legal question, but it is a reason to pause, investigate, and revise or replace the material before sharing it.

Important Current Guidance: Using AI does not always remove copyright protection. A person's writing, design choices, or major changes may still be protected. Work made only by AI is usually not protected, and a prompt alone may not show enough human work. Follow current school, workplace, and copyright rules.

Student rule: Do not assume AI output is free to use. Identify the source, check permission and license terms, add meaningful original work, give required credit, and follow school or workplace rules. Ask a qualified instructor, supervisor, or responsible person when the answer is unclear.

Key terms and practical definitions for copyright, permissions, and AI disclosure
Key Term What It Means in Practice
Permission or License Explains whether material may be used, changed, or shared and what rules must be followed.
Attribution Gives credit to the original creator and source as required by the license, terms, or professional rules.
AI-Use Disclosure Explains how AI helped and what meaningful work a person completed.

💡 Essential Reminder:

Giving credit does not replace permission. Explaining AI use does not replace crediting outside sources.

Real-World Scenarios in Action:

  • Unacceptable Use: A student uses AI to make an entire graphic. The student makes no changes, submits it as original work, and does not explain that AI was used.
  • Responsible Use: A student asks AI for logo ideas. The student redraws and edits one idea in design software. They credit the source of the idea and explain how they used AI and changed the design.

Learn: Create Responsibly with AI

Estimated time: 8 minutes

Responsible AI Routine: Goal → Protect → Use → Check → Own

▶️ Your next move: Watch the video below. Focus on the ideas you will use in this step.

Viewing Focus:

English transcript | Watch in Spanish | Watch in Arabic

📖 Your next move: Continue with the short reading and examples below.

A responsible creator uses AI as an assistant, not as a hidden replacement. AI may help list ideas, suggest a structure, or give feedback. The person must add meaningful ideas, choices, writing, design, code, skilled work, or revision. The person should also be able to explain the final decisions.

Do not ask AI to copy a specific artist, classmate, company, or brand. Instead, describe the features you need, such as a calm tone, clear labels, or a simple layout. Asking for the exact style of a living artist raises ethical and professional concerns about originality. It is not automatically a copyright violation. A logo that looks too much like an existing brand may also create trademark concerns.

Check the final work before using or sharing it. Compare important text, images, code, and claims with trusted sources. Search for close matches when needed. A search is only one check; it does not prove that the work is original, allowed, correct, or safe. Remove copied or unsupported parts, fix mistakes, and keep notes or drafts that show your own work.

Table: Key responsibilities for ethical and transparent AI use
Responsibility What It Explains / Accomplishes
AI-Use Disclosure Explains how the AI tool helped with the creation process.
Source Attribution Credits outside creators and materials referenced or used.
Permission / License Decides whether outside materials may be legally used or modified.
Human Contribution Explains the important choices, changes, and checks completed by a person.

💡 Essential Reminder:

Each creator responsibility is separate and important. Clearly explain how AI helped and follow all specific rules from your teacher, school, client, or employer.

The PAUSE Creator Check

P — Purpose, People, and Reason: What is being created? Why is AI being used? Who may benefit, and who could be harmed, left out, copied, or misled? What role should AI have?

A — Authorization and Rights: Who created or owns the source material? Where did it come from? What permission, license, contract, or terms apply? Is the planned use allowed? Does the user have permission to upload it?

U — Use AI as an Assistant: What meaningful ideas, choices, writing, design, code, or skilled work will a person add? Can the creator explain and support the final decisions?

S — Search, Check, and Strengthen: Check the facts, safety, sources, rules, and possible copied material. Change, replace, or reject anything you cannot confirm.

E — Explain, Credit, and Evaluate: Credit allowed sources correctly. Provide a separate AI-use disclosure. Identify the human work, choose Approve, Revise, or Reject, and name the person with final authority.

Real-World Scenarios in Action:

  • Unacceptable Use: A student asks AI to copy the style of code from a developer's private online project. The student submits the code without changes, says it is all their own work, and does not mention using AI.
  • Responsible Use: A student asks AI for layout ideas, then changes the design by hand. They use a reverse image search to look for close matches. They also explain how AI helped with the project.
