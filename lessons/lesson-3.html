Lesson 3: Verify Before You Trust: Auditing AI Media

Step 3.11 of 6

Step 3.1 · Start here

AI can create realistic images, audio, video, graphics, logos, and designs. But realistic media may still be wrong, unsafe, fake, or not suitable to use.

In this lesson, you will study workplace-style images. You will see how prompts and training patterns shape AI images and why detailed prompts do not promise safe or correct results. You will use Pause, Trace, Check, Decide, Disclose to review made-up workplace media. Then you will choose Approve, Revise, or Reject and support your choice with evidence.

💡 Big Question and Core Rule

Big Question: How can I check media made or changed by AI? How can I decide whether it is safe and fair to use or share?

Main Rule: Do not decide based only on whether media looks real or AI-generated. Trace its , check its accuracy and context, evaluate its impact, and then decide whether it is responsible to use or share.

🧠 Memory Phrase: Pause. Trace. Check. Decide. Disclose.

🛡️ Use the Responsible AI Routine

Use this pathway whenever AI may support a task.

When to use this routine

Use it before AI supports a school, workplace, or personal task, especially when information, safety, fairness, or other people may be affected.

  • Protect: What private, copyrighted, sensitive, or identifying information must be protected?

  • Own: Who is responsible for approving, revising, rejecting, or sharing the final media?

🎯 Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, students can:

  • Explain how prompts, training patterns, and model limitations shape AI-generated images.
  • Explain how a media file's source differs from whether it is real, correct, or shown in context. Use Pause, Trace, Check, Decide, Disclose to find the proof you need.
  • Name a needed fix, safety step, source credit, AI-use statement, or human approval.
  • Check who is shown, who may be affected, and what safety or disclosure steps are needed. Use evidence to choose Approve, Revise, or Reject.
✅ You will show success by: explaining how prompts and patterns shape AI media, distinguishing from accuracy and responsible use, identifying credible evidence, evaluating community effects, recommending a safeguard, and justifying an Approve, Revise, or Reject decision.

Could You Tell for Certain?

Some AI-generated images have obvious mistakes, while others look polished. Real photos can also be blurry, edited, cropped, poorly lit, or paired with a misleading caption. Looks alone cannot prove that media is real, accurate, or responsible to use.

A visual clue can begin an investigation, but it is not proof. Reviewers trace where the media came from, check its claims and context, and consider who may be affected. Then they decide whether to approve, revise, reject, or label the media.

📖 Your next move: Read the three evaluation questions and 5-step process below.

3 Key Questions Before Trusting Media:

  • & Authenticity: Where did it come from, who created it, and how was it changed?
  • Accuracy & Context: Are the image, caption, event, measurements, and claims correct?
  • Responsible Use & Impact: Is it fair, safe, allowed, right for the audience, and clearly labeled?

The 5-Step Media Check:

  1. Pause: Do not share or approve media only because it looks convincing.
  2. Trace: Find the original source, creator, caption, date, context, credits, and edit history/Content Credentials.
  3. Check: Compare claims with trusted sources, workplace rules, specifications, and experts. Verify safety, fairness, privacy, and permissions.
  4. Decide: Choose Approve, Revise, or Reject based on evidence and potential impact.
  5. Disclose: Clearly explain or label AI use when required by policy or platform rules.

Note: An AI detector score or visual flaw is a clue, not definitive proof. Neither should be used as the sole evidence for a decision.
