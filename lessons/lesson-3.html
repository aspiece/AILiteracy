Lesson 3

Verify Before You Trust — Auditing AI Media

Step 3.11 of 6

Step 3.1 · Start here

AI can create realistic images, audio, video, graphics, logos, and designs. But realistic media may still be wrong, unsafe, fake, or not suitable to use.

In this lesson, you will study workplace-style images. You will see how prompts and training patterns shape AI images and why detailed prompts do not promise safe or correct results. You will use Pause, Trace, Check, Decide, Disclose to review made-up workplace media. Then you will choose Approve, Revise, or Reject and support your choice with evidence.

💡 Big Question and Core Rule

Big Question: How can I check media made or changed by AI? How can I decide whether it is safe and fair to use or share?

Main Rule: Do not decide based only on whether media looks real or AI-generated. Trace its provenance, check its accuracy and context, evaluate its impact, and then decide whether it is responsible to use or share.

🧠 Memory Phrase: Pause. Trace. Check. Decide. Disclose.

🛡️ Use the Responsible AI Routine

Use this pathway whenever AI may support a task.

  • Protect: What private, copyrighted, sensitive, or identifying information must be protected?
  • Use: What purpose, audience, and limits should guide the media?
  • Check: What claims, sources, safety requirements, technical details, and professional standards must be verified?
  • Own: Who is responsible for approving, revising, rejecting, or sharing the final media?

🎯 Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, students can:

  • Explain how prompts, training patterns, and model limitations shape AI-generated images.
  • Explain how a media file's source differs from whether it is real, correct, or shown in context. Use Pause, Trace, Check, Decide, Disclose to find the proof you need.
  • Name a needed fix, safety step, source credit, AI-use statement, or human approval.
  • Check who is shown, who may be affected, and what safety or disclosure steps are needed. Use evidence to choose Approve, Revise, or Reject.
✅ You will show success by: explaining how prompts and patterns shape AI media, distinguishing provenance from accuracy and responsible use, identifying credible evidence, evaluating community effects, recommending a safeguard, and justifying an Approve, Revise, or Reject decision.

📚 Key Words to Know

AI Image Generation & Prompts
AI Image Generator: A tool that creates images from text prompts by using patterns learned from training data.
Prompt: A written description that tells an AI image generator what to create.
Prompt Refinement: Revising a prompt by adding, removing, or changing details to improve the result.
Training Data: Examples used to help an AI system learn patterns between words and visual features.
Diffusion Model: A type of image model that begins with random noise and gradually changes it into an image.
Verification, Media & Ethics
AI-Generated Media: Images, video, or audio created or substantially changed by AI.
Deepfake: AI-created or edited media that makes a real person appear to say or do something that did not happen.
Misinformation: False or wrong information that may mislead people. (Disinformation is false information shared on purpose to deceive).
Provenance: Information that shows where media came from, who created it, and how it was changed.
Verification: Checking media by using its original source, trusted evidence, official information, workplace rules, or qualified experts.

Could You Tell for Certain?

Some AI-generated images have obvious mistakes, while others look polished. Real photos can also be blurry, edited, cropped, poorly lit, or paired with a misleading caption. Looks alone cannot prove that media is real, accurate, or responsible to use.

A visual clue can begin an investigation, but it is not proof. Reviewers trace where the media came from, check its claims and context, and consider who may be affected. Then they decide whether to approve, revise, reject, or label the media.

📖 Your next move: Read the short excerpt below. Focus on how AI image prompts work.

An AI image prompt tells the tool what you want it to create. A strong prompt may name the main subject, background, colors, style, mood, lighting, and point of view. The tool uses these details to create visual features that are likely to fit the request.

The first image may not match your goal. Improve the prompt by adding or removing details. You can change the angle, lighting, mood, or focus. Small changes may create large differences. Treat each result as a draft and keep revising until it fits the purpose.

📖 Your next move: Continue with the short reading and examples below.

An AI image generator creates images from text prompts. During training, it learns patterns that connect words with visual features such as objects, colors, styles, lighting, mood, and camera angle. The system does not imagine or understand the scene like a person. It creates features that are likely to match the prompt.

The result depends on the training data, the system’s design, and the prompt. Missing or inaccurate patterns may cause mistakes or stereotypes. A detailed prompt may improve the image, but it cannot guarantee readable words, correct measurements, safe steps, fair representation, original work, or permission to use the result.

📖 Your next move: Read the three evaluation questions and 5-step process below.

3 Key Questions Before Trusting Media:

  • Provenance & Authenticity: Where did it come from, who created it, and how was it changed?
  • Accuracy & Context: Are the image, caption, event, measurements, and claims correct?
  • Responsible Use & Impact: Is it fair, safe, allowed, right for the audience, and clearly labeled?

The 5-Step Media Check:

  1. Pause: Do not share or approve media only because it looks convincing.
  2. Trace: Find the original source, creator, caption, date, context, credits, and edit history/Content Credentials.
  3. Check: Compare claims with trusted sources, workplace rules, specifications, and experts. Verify safety, fairness, privacy, and permissions.
  4. Decide: Choose Approve, Revise, or Reject based on evidence and potential impact.
  5. Disclose: Clearly explain or label AI use when required by policy or platform rules.

Note: An AI detector score or visual flaw is a clue, not definitive proof. Neither should be used as the sole evidence for a decision.
