Step 7.1 · Start here

AI is changing careers, industries, and daily tasks. It can do some routine tasks and help people with others. But using AI also involves choices. People must weigh speed and accuracy against human review, energy use, and whether the tool fits the task.

This lesson looks at how AI may change careers. You will compare , where AI does a task, with , where AI helps a person do a task. You will study the benefits and costs of using AI, including its effects on energy and the environment. You will also identify the that still matter at work.

💡 Big Question and Core Rule

Big Question: How can people and workplaces use AI and still keep important ? How can they lower its effects on energy, resources, and daily work?

Main Rule: Choose the right tool for the right task. Use AI to augment capabilities and automate routine steps, but keep human judgment, creativity, and empathy at the core of decision-making.

🧠 Memory Phrase: Augment with AI, lead with human skill.

While you watch, find three things. Why does your own knowledge matter? How might AI help you learn? What rule would make that use safer?

AI can act like a tutor. It can change an explanation or practice activity based on what a learner needs. It can also make examples, simulations, or language practice. These tools may help, but they do not help every student in the same way. Their value depends on the learning goal, the quality of the answer, fair access, privacy, and support from a teacher or other adult.

Your own knowledge still matters. It helps you ask better questions, spot missing details, and compare an AI answer with trusted evidence. Critical thinking helps you decide whether to use, fix, combine, or reject an AI answer.

The video says AI may make personal knowledge more important. Think about that claim. What evidence supports it? When could too much trust in AI, poor answers, limited access, or weak support reduce the benefit?

Important context: AI will affect tasks, careers, workplaces, and regions in different ways. Many jobs may change as certain tasks change. Some workers may lose opportunities or need to move into different roles. New jobs are possible, but they are not guaranteed.

AI may change parts of a job without removing the whole job. It can help with simple tasks such as sorting facts, making a schedule, finding patterns, or writing a first draft. People may then spend more time solving hard problems. They may also work with customers or patients, make safety choices, lead teams, and do hands-on work.

Some careers may grow, shrink, or change. New jobs may appear for people who train, test, fix, review, secure, or manage AI systems. These changes are possible, but they are not certain. Students can prepare by building job skills, communication, good judgment, flexibility, and a habit of learning new things.

Step 7.4 · Learn and check

Learn: Societal, Access, and Environmental Impacts

Estimated time: 11 minutes

AI can create benefits and costs at the same time. Your job is to examine the full impact before deciding whether a use is responsible.

▶️ Your next move: Watch for one AI challenge that may affect groups of people differently.

English transcript | Watch in Spanish | Watch in Arabic

Use four impact questions

1. Work and skills

What time could be saved? What skills could grow, weaken, or change?

2. People and fairness

Who benefits? Who could be harmed or excluded? Who can question a result?

3. Access

Can people use the tool across devices, languages, disabilities, costs, and training levels?

4. Resources and environment

What energy, cooling water, equipment, and replacement waste may be required?

Important: Impact estimates change with the model, task, hardware, data center, power source, and amount of use. Use estimates for comparison, not as exact measurements.

Try the Everyday AI Impact calculator

  1. Open the calculator.
  2. Choose one AI activity and record the estimate for one use.
  3. Change the amount to 2,000 uses and compare the energy, phone-battery, and water estimates.
  4. Name one safeguard that could reduce unnecessary use or improve access.

Make a responsible recommendation

State the benefit, the most important cost or risk, one safeguard, and the trained person who should review the result.

Decision Guide Reference

Use these definitions when choosing your recommendation level in Part 5 of the worksheet:

  • Green Light: AI adds clear value, risks can be managed, a simpler method does not work as well, and specific human review stays in place.
  • Yellow Light: AI may help, but some concerns must be fixed first. Check the limits, review steps, training needs, access, and effects on resources.
  • Red Light: The use creates unacceptable concerns about safety, privacy, fairness, law, ethics, assessment, or resources. A simpler method may also meet the need more responsibly.
