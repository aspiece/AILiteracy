💡 Big Question and Core Rule

Big Question: How can people preserve the useful purpose of an AI task while protecting information throughout its lifecycle?

Main Rule: Use only the information you need. Make sure you have permission and follow the approved steps. An approved tool does not mean you may enter every detail.

🧠 Memory Phrase: When in doubt, leave it out.

Learn: What Should I Share with AI? (5 minutes)

English transcript | Watch in Spanish | Watch in Arabic

📖 Your next move: Continue with the short reading and examples below.

Watch for examples of information that is generally safe, unsafe, or dependent on context.

Some information is general and does not identify a person. A broad interest, grade level, public fact, or fictional practice example may be safe when it does not reveal someone’s identity. Names, addresses, account numbers, photos, and voice recordings are direct identifiers. Other details, such as an exact date, rare condition, job title, location, or project name, may reveal a person when combined.

Information may belong to other people or organizations. Do not share a classmate’s photo, a customer record, an employer document, or someone else’s creative work just because you can access it. Access does not mean ownership or permission. Policies, contracts, and professional rules still apply.

A useful first decision is:

  • Clear — The request uses an approved tool and only allowed, public, fictional, general, or minimum-needed information.
  • Warning — The task may be safe after all identifying details are removed. Do not include protected records, passwords, private work, or restricted material.
  • Stop — The request needs protected records, passwords, evidence, trade secrets, private code, confidential client work, or other restricted information without permission. Also stop when tool approval or user permission is unknown. Ask a qualified person before continuing.

Watch what happens to at each step. may be entered, used, saved, kept, reviewed, or shared. A company may also use it to train or improve a product. At the end, the may be deleted, or a person may ask for it to be deleted.

Entering information is only the first choice. Follow the through each step: enter, use, save, keep, review or share, and delete. A company might also use the to train or improve a product.

Processing means the service studies the text, image, file, or recording you submit to create a result. Storage and retention describe where the information is kept and for how long. Employees, contractors, connected services, or organization leaders may review or receive it. Deleting a visible chat does not prove that saved or backup copies are gone.

Some services may use information for safety checks, product improvement, or model training. Approved school or workplace accounts may have different contract protections. The rules depend on the tool, account, settings, organization, contract, and policy. Do not assume every AI service handles the same way.

A safe workplace habit is to assume that information entered into a public or unapproved AI tool could leave the workplace. Do not rely only on a delete button, a promise from the chatbot, or removing a last name.

Watch for strategies involving controls, account management, and policies.

People cannot control every choice a technology company makes, but they can reduce risk.

controls may let you turn off access to your location, camera, microphone, history, or files. They may also limit how a company uses your . Protect your account with a strong password and two-step verification. Sign out of shared devices, keep track of your accounts, and delete accounts you no longer use.

policies explain what a tool says it collects, stores, shares, sells, or uses for training. They may explain how to request deletion, download your , opt out, or contact the company. School and workplace rules may be stricter than the tool’s public policy.

Settings do not make unsafe information safe. Private or temporary modes may save less history, but you must still use only the minimum needed , confirm permission, and follow the approved process. An approved tool is not permission to upload every record, file, or detail you can access.
